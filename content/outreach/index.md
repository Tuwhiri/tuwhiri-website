      text: |-
        Building the instrument is only part of the work. A long-term climate
        monitoring capability for Aotearoa needs people trained to build and use
        it, industry able to manufacture it, and a public that understands why it
        matters.

        ### Public and school engagement

        With Otago Museum we bring atmospheric science and photonics to school
        students and the wider public — exhibitions, school programmes and
        events that show what an ozone measurement is and why the Antarctic
        stratosphere matters to New Zealand weather. A particular focus is
        opening pathways into science for Māori and Pacific learners.

        ### Industry partnership

        We work with New Zealand companies on manufacture, commercialisation and
        deployment, so the capability we develop here can be built and sold from
        here rather than exported as an idea.

        ### Growing the workforce

        Tuwhiri supports doctoral and masters students across all three research
        areas. Their training is part of the programme's purpose, not a
        by-product of it: the people who work on this instrument are the people
        who will run the missions that follow.
